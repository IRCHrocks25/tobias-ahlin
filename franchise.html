The Trust Gap

You've spent years building relationships in your city. People know you. They trust you. That's not something I can teach you, and it's not something most AI consultants will ever have.

What you don't have yet is a proven way to turn that trust into a repeatable, profitable service. Most people trying to break into AI automation are guessing their way through it, hoping something works. That's not a business. That's a hobby with better marketing.

I built a method that already works. Not in theory. In an actual company, saving actual hours, this year. You bring the relationships. I bring the method. Neither one works as well alone.

The Origin Story

Why a Stranger in Another City Reached Out First

I didn't build a franchise pitch and go looking for buyers. Someone found me first.

Twenty years ago I started selling to the world's most demanding data companies. Betting operators, gaming platforms, banks. In the last ten years of it alone, I sold over 30 million dollars of the most expensive software there is and made President's Club five times. At Snowflake, my last stop, I watched the company grow from 350 million dollars to 2.6 billion while I was there.

Somewhere in there my body started failing before I noticed. Bad kidneys, a bad liver, high cholesterol. All from stress. In the same stretch of years I lost nine people close to me to cancer.

My wife's youngest sister. A colleague I'd worked beside for years. My stepbrother.

Nine people. I had the money to stop. I didn't know how much time I had left to use it. So in April 2023 I walked away, on my own terms, at 42.

I wrote about all of it openly on LinkedIn. Not a sales page, just the truth. A month and a half ago, an entrepreneur in another city read it and reached out. He wasn't responding to a pitch. He was responding to something true.

That conversation is now a six-figure deal, verbally agreed. Not signed yet. But real enough that I know this works beyond my own city.

The Market

Your City Is Already Waiting

I'm not interested in Stockholm, Gothenburg, or Malmö for this. Those cities already have local AI software partners fighting over the same clients. I looked instead at cities of 50,000 to 75,000 people, the size I live near myself.

Ninety percent of companies in Sweden are small. Construction, manufacturing, plumbing, distribution. Nobody is calling them. Not the big consultancies, not the boutique AI shops, not anyone. That's not a smaller opportunity. It's a wide open one, in a market where you already have the relationships to walk through the door first.

I've identified roughly ten cities like this so far, matched on two things: the population range, and no local AI or automation partner already operating there. Yours might already be one of them.
